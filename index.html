Footer · IA v3 · AEO-Ready

Footer architecture for the AI-search era.

A documented recommendation for the global footer on demandspring.com. Designed to do three jobs at once — orient lost visitors, distribute SEO link equity, and surface citable facts that AI engines extract verbatim. Annotations explain the reasoning behind every part.

Three jobs the footer needs to do
  1. Navigation safety net For visitors who scroll looking for something specific the primary nav doesn't surface.
  2. SEO link equity Pushes authority to deeper pages that didn't earn primary-nav real estate.
  3. AEO citation surface Structured "who is this company" facts AI engines can extract and cite verbatim.

Why each part is there

      
Ten decisions, explained.

      
Each card maps to a numbered pin on the mock above. Tags note the primary discipline driving the choice — AEO (AI engine optimization), SEO (search rankings), CONV (conversion), UX, or A11Y (accessibility).

    


    

      


        

          
1

          

            
Trust bar — "fact tape"

            
Credentials styled as cited evidence, not promo banner.

            
Dark band, monospace, ALL CAPS, +0.14em tracking, dot separators. Reads as machine-extractable fact tape — visually distinct from the link columns below it.

            

              Why this works for AEO
              Reinforces the same credentials the JSON-LD asserts (Marketo Preferred Partner, Forbes Business Council, NPS 98, founding year). When the visible page agrees with the structured data, AI engines treat the facts as high-confidence and cite them more readily.
            

            

              AEO
              Trust
            

          

        


        

          
2

          

            
5-column structure

            
Selective columns, not a sitemap dump.

            
Five columns — Company, Solutions, Services, Insights & Events, Connect — each carrying 4–10 of the deepest-earning pages, not all 24+ services or every sub-page. Deliberate selectivity preserves authority signal per link.

            
    
              
  • Services: picked the 10 with search demand or SEO clustering value (Marketo, HubSpot, Eloqua, ABM, AI Search Visibility, etc.)
    • 
              
  • Solutions: mirrors primary nav so each solution page gets a second link-equity push
    • 
              
  • Company: Careers stays linked — third-largest landing page per GA, 88 sessions in 90d
    • 
            

            

              Why this works for SEO
              Diluted footers dilute authority. A footer with 200+ links spreads PageRank thin and risks looking like a link farm. 30–50 carefully chosen links concentrate equity on the pages that actually need to rank.
            

            

              SEO
              IA
            

          

        


        

          
3

          

            
Hub anchor links

            
Bold "Solutions" / "Services" entries with green dot.

            
The first item in Solutions and Services columns links to the hub page itself, weighted bold with a green-square indicator. Clear visual signal that this is the parent, not a peer.

            

              Why this works for SEO & UX
              Hub pages are usually the strongest internal-rank-target on the site. Making them visually prominent in the footer creates an additional internal link with descriptive anchor text ("Solutions", "Services") that reinforces topical authority.
            

            

              SEO
              UX
            

          

        


        

          
4

          

            
Connect column

            
Conversion path, not link list.

            
The 5th column breaks pattern intentionally. It anchors with the primary "Let's Talk" CTA (pill button, ink fill, white text — matches the hero CTA treatment), then offers progressively softer engagement: newsletter signup, then social channels, then Case Studies as a footer-only ghost button.

            

              Why this works for conversion
              The footer is the second-most-scrolled element on most marketing sites. Offering a tiered ladder of commitment (call → subscribe → follow → browse proof) captures visitors who scrolled past the hero CTA without converting.
            

            

              CONV
              UX
            

          

        


        

          
5

          

            
Newsletter signup

            
Inline, single-field, with promise of value.

            
Single email input + Join button. Helper line below ("Monthly. Strategy briefings, research, no fluff.") sets explicit expectation — frequency, content type, no spam.

            

              Why this works
              Footer signups convert ~3× better when they tell visitors what they're agreeing to. "Subscribe" alone is 2010 thinking; "monthly strategy briefings" is what enterprise marketers actually want to know before handing over an email.
            

            

              CONV
              UX
            

          

        


        

          
6

          

            
Case Studies — ghost button

            
Footer-only entry, weighted as a button.

            
Per the GA-backed IA decision, Case Studies live in the footer rather than primary nav. Styled as a ghost button (ink border, hover swaps to filled ink) so it doesn't read as a muted afterthought next to the primary CTA above it.

            

              Why this works
              When something is the only path to a content type, it has to look like the path. A muted text link tells visitors "this is incidental"; a button tells them "this is intentional".
            

            

              CONV
              UX
            

          

        


        

          
7

          

            
Brand strip placement

            
Identity anchors below the navigation, not above.

            
Logo, positioning paragraph, offices, and contact info sit beneath the link columns rather than above them. Visitors scroll into navigation first; the brand info is the closing signature.

            

              Why this flows better
              Putting the logo first repeats the header — visual redundancy. Putting it last gives the footer a clear reading rhythm: navigate → identify → legal. It's the same pattern as the back of a business letter.
            

            

              UX
              AEO
            

          

        


        

          
8

          

            
Office locations

            
Four named cities, even though the company is virtual-first.

            
Ottawa, Toronto, Boston, Vancouver — listed as named locations with region/country, not as a single "virtual" line.

            

              Why this works for AEO & local SEO
              Named places become Place schema entries (see annotation 10), which AI engines use to answer location-qualified queries like "Marketo consultants in Boston" or "B2B marketing automation Canada". A single "virtual" line forfeits all of that.
            

            

              AEO
              Local SEO
            

          

        


        

          
9

          

            
Utility / legal row

            
Five required items, no more.

            
Copyright, Privacy Policy, Terms of Service, Cookie Settings, Accessibility Statement (WCAG 2.1 AA), Sitemap. Single horizontal strip below the green hairline rule.

            
    
              
  • Cookie Settings opens the consent manager; required by GDPR/CCPA.
    • 
              
  • Accessibility Statement is a one-page declaration of intent — even without a formal audit, it's worth having.
    • 
              
  • Sitemap is the HTML version; /sitemap.xml is referenced separately in robots.txt.
    • 
            

            

              Why this works for SEO & A11Y
              A linked HTML sitemap gives crawlers a deterministic path to every page. The accessibility statement signals WCAG awareness to procurement reviewers — many enterprise buyers screen for it before issuing an RFP.
            

            

              SEO
              A11Y
            

          

        


        

          
10

          

            
JSON-LD schema block

            
The invisible AEO half of the work.

            
A <script type="application/ld+json"> in the global footer template emits Organization, ContactPoint, Place, Award, and aggregateRating schema. Most of the AEO value lives here, not in the visible links.

            
    
              
  • Organization — legal name, founded 2012, founder Mark Emond, logo, sameAs (LinkedIn / X / YouTube / Crunchbase)
    • 
              
  • ContactPoint — customer service, phone, email, areas served (US, CA), languages (en, fr)
    • 
              
  • Place — Ottawa, Toronto, Boston, Vancouver as named locations
    • 
              
  • Award — Adobe Marketo Preferred Partner, Forbes Business Council
    • 
              
  • aggregateRating — NPS 98 over 500 ratings as a citable score
    • 
            

            

              Why this is non-negotiable for AEO
              Without structured data, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews have to infer who Demand Spring is from prose — and they get it wrong, conflate the brand, or skip the citation entirely. With it, they extract ground truth verbatim. This is the single highest-leverage AEO investment on the entire site, and it's invisible.
            

            

              AEO
              SEO
            

          

        


      

    


    
    

      
Two more things worth flagging.

      

        

          
What we're deliberately NOT doing

          
    
            
  • Mirroring every nav item. 4–10 deepest pages per column; diluted footer = diluted authority.
    • 
            
  • "Learn more" link text. Every link uses the actual page name — better for SEO, A11Y, and AEO.
    • 
            
  • Keyword-stuffed paragraphs. No "leading B2B Marketo HubSpot Eloqua…" prose. Google penalised this for years.
    • 
            
  • Linking gated assets directly. Reports point to landing pages; the landing page handles the gate.
    • 
            
  • Footer-only orphaned pages that belong in primary nav (and vice versa).
    •
